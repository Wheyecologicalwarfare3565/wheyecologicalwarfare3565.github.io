📌 What This Tool Does

wardogs_tool is a small desktop companion for the WARDOGS game, built for people who spend their matches building, fortifying and supporting their team with indirect fire. If you have ever held down the hammer key and felt like your character was swinging in slow motion, or if you have ever guessed a mortar angle and watched the shell land in the wrong trench, this program was made for you.

The application runs on Windows in its own window. It sits beside the game, gives you a handful of clearly labelled controls, and lets you switch features on and off whenever you want. There is nothing to configure in text files, no command line, and no developer tools required. You download it, you open it, you pick the options you like, and you go back to playing.

Everything in the program is grouped around four everyday construction problems: hitting a hammer faster than the normal animation allows, working out where a mortar round will actually land, seeing distant details at the centre of your view, and staying active long enough to keep your place in a match without being kicked for being idle.

⚡ Fast Hammer: Speed Up Every Swing

The fast hammer feature is the heart of this tool. In normal play, every hammer strike follows the same animation, and that animation takes time. When you are trying to raise a wall before an enemy squad pushes your position, those extra fractions of a second matter. The fast hammer module repeats the strike action rapidly so your character keeps hammering without you having to press the key over and over.

Three hammer sizes are supported, because they are not used the same way. The large hammer is for heavy structural work and thick walls. The medium hammer is the all-round choice most players keep in their hands while moving between build sites. The small hammer is quicker by nature and suits fine detail work and repairs. You choose the size that matches what you are holding in the game, then turn the speed on. You can also change the speed itself, so you can start with a gentle pace while you get a feel for it and increase it once you are comfortable.

Because building in WARDOGS is not only about walls, the fast hammer also helps with the smaller jobs: stacking sandbags, placing planks, repairing damaged sections and finishing emplacements under fire. Instead of clicking dozens of times, you hold your position and let the tool keep the rhythm steady.

🎯 Mortar Calculator: Direction, Distance, Azimuth

Mortars are the part of WARDOGS that punish guesswork the hardest. A few degrees off on the tube and the round lands thirty metres from where you wanted it. The built-in mortar calculator takes the maths out of your hands and gives you a clear answer.

You enter the position of your mortar and the position of the target, or the distance between them if you already know it. The calculator returns three things. Direction tells you which way the tube should be pointed relative to the way you are facing. Distance tells you how far the target is in metres, so you can judge whether the shot is inside your useful range. Azimuth gives you the compass bearing in degrees, which is what most experienced mortar crews actually call out to each other.

The result updates instantly as you change the numbers, so you can compare two firing spots without writing anything down. Settle on the position with the better angle, dial the numbers in, and drop the round where your team needs it. No spreadsheets, no mental arithmetic in the middle of a fight.

🔍 Screen Center Magnifier and Zoom

Sometimes the thing you need to see is tiny: a figure moving between two distant buildings, a wire you are trying to line up, or the exact pixel where your mortar tube is pointing. The magnifier enlarges a region around the centre of your screen so those details become readable.

The zoom level is adjustable, so you can use a light enlargement that keeps your surroundings visible or a stronger scope-like view for long-range spotting. The enlarged area is drawn in a small window of its own, which means you can keep it pinned in a corner and glance at it while still watching the rest of the battlefield.

⏱️ Anti-AFK: Stay in the Round

Long build sessions and slow defensive phases leave a lot of players standing still. Anti-AFK sends a small, harmless input at a steady interval so the game sees continued activity and keeps you connected. You set how often it happens, switch it on when you expect a quiet stretch, and switch it off when the action returns.
